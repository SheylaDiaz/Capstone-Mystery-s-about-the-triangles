The Alaska Triangle.

The Alaska Triangle covers a large portion of the state, starting at the southern coast with Juneau and Anchorage. The triangle is completed by Utqiagvik along the northern coast.This massive expanse of land includes steep mountains, hidden caves, treacherous bodies of water, and dense forests. The terrain is unforgiving, with intense snowstorms hitting the area throughout the year.Considering the size of the Alaska Triangle, it’s not surprising that people go missing. What is surprising is how they go missing without a trace despite months-long searches. Rumors of cryptids and aliens only further fuel the speculation that something supernatural controls the environment of the Alaska Triangle.

The Bermuda Triangle.

The Bermuda Triangle, also known as the Devil's Triangle, is a loosely defined region in the North Atlantic Ocean, roughly bounded by Florida, Bermuda, and Puerto Rico. Since the mid-20th century, it has been the focus of an urban legend suggesting that many aircraft, ships, and people have disappeared there under mysterious circumstances.

Lake Michigan Triangle

The Lake Michigan Triangle is, according to modern legends, an area of Lake Michigan where a number of disappearances, shipwrecks, and plane crashes have occurred under unexplained circumstances. Unidentified flying objects (UFOs) and unidentified submerged objects (USOs), have also allegedly been spotted in the area. The triangle stretches from Ludington to Manitowoc, down to Benton Harbor, and back to Ludington.

Dragon's Triangle (Devil's Sea)

The Devil's Sea (Japanese: 魔の海, Hepburn: Ma no Umi), also known as the Devil's triangle, the Dragon's Triangle, the Formosa Triangle and the Pacific Bermuda Triangle, is a region of the Pacific, south of Tokyo. The Devil's Sea is sometimes considered a paranormal location, though the veracity of these claims has been questioned.

Bridgewater Triangle (Massachusetts)

The Bridgewater Triangle is an area of about 200 square miles (520 km2) within southeastern Massachusetts in the United States, claimed to be a site of alleged paranormal phenomena, ranging from UFOs to poltergeists, and other spectral phenomena, various bigfoot-like sightings, giant snakes and thunderbirds. The term was coined by New England-based cryptozoologist Loren Coleman.

Bennington Triangle

The Bennington Triangle is a mysterious area in southwestern Vermont known for a series of unexplained disappearances, strange sightings, and paranormal stories, especially between the 1940s and 1950s. It’s one of the lesser-known “triangles” but has some of the creepiest cases.

Nevada Triangle

The Nevada Triangle is a region in the western United States where a high number of aircraft disappearances and crashes have been reported. It’s often compared to the Bermuda Triangle, but instead of ocean mysteries, this one is mostly about aviation incidents over land.

Bass strait triangle

The Bass Strait Triangle is a, reputedly, notorious area between mainland Australia and Tasmania, often dubbed "Australia's Bermuda Triangle" due to numerous disappearances of ships and aircraft. Known for dangerous weather, shallow waters, and unpredictable currents, the area has hosted many unexplained incidents since the 1800s, most notably the 1978 disappearance of pilot Frederick Valentich.
